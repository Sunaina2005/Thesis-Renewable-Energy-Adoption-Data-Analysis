Key Finding: The GDP–RE relationship shows a strong positive correlation (r = 0.71), but with important nuances. High-income countries lead in per-capita terms, yet China and India (upper-middle income) dominate absolute capacity due to scale. Panel regression with fixed effects yields a GDP elasticity of 1.24 (p < 0.001), suggesting RE investment scales super-proportionally with economic growth. However, policy variables (FIT, RPS) add significant explanatory power beyond GDP alone (ΔR² = +0.18).

Interpretation: SHAP analysis reveals that GDP per capita is the single strongest predictor of RE adoption (mean |SHAP| = 0.34), followed closely by policy instruments (Feed-in Tariffs, RPS) at 0.28. Notably, carbon price shows an outsized effect relative to its prevalence—countries with carbon pricing mechanisms adopt RE at 2.3× the rate of comparable peers. The interaction of GDP × Policy is superadditive: high-income countries with strong policy frameworks show SHAP values 3× higher than would be expected from either factor alone.
